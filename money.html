Post-Arrival

Money & Taxes

Banking, budgeting, and the tax side of being an assistant. This page is general guidance only — not professional financial or tax advice.

Banking

Most landlords require a French bank account before signing a lease, so opening one is worth prioritizing early. By law, schools must deposit your pay into any EU bank account — they cannot require it to be specifically French.

Keep a fee-free international card from home for the transition period, and withdraw French cash as needed once your account is set up. French consumer banking protections can differ from what you may be used to, so if your card is ever lost or stolen: block it immediately in your banking app, contact your bank, and file a police report right away — all three steps matter for any chance of reimbursement.

Banque Populaire

  • Commonly used by assistants, sometimes recommended directly by schools
  • Experiences with French-language requirements can vary by branch

HSBC

  • Geared toward international customers, generally easier in English
  • Account setup can take longer, and monthly fees may apply
Asking your Professeur Référent to accompany you to a bank appointment is often the fastest way to get approved for an account.

If several banks decline you, you have a legal right to a basic account ("droit au compte"): go to the Banque de France with proof of your rejections, and they will assign you a bank that cannot refuse you. More info here.

For transferring money between a home-country account and your French one, a dedicated currency-transfer service (rather than a standard bank wire) often gives a better exchange rate and lower fees — worth comparing options before choosing one.

Budget

How much you need varies enormously by lifestyle, region, and rent — there's no single number that fits everyone. As a general reality check: some assistants find that saving more than the minimum commonly-cited buffer gives real peace of mind, especially once you factor in temporary housing while you search for a permanent place. Reaching out to a mentor or community member already living in your specific region is the most reliable way to get a realistic estimate for your situation.

Important: you won't receive your first month's salary until the end of October or even November — budget for this gap. Additionally, that October payment (when it arrives) is typically around 70% of a normal month, with November making up the difference at around 130% — though this can vary by academy. Plan your early months around this uneven schedule.

Credit Cards

Having an international credit card with no foreign transaction fees is worth having as a backup, since it can take time for your first payment to arrive. Some travel rewards cards also let you build up points toward a flight home. Note that Discover and American Express are often not accepted in France — a Visa or Mastercard is a safer default.

Taxes

This section is general guidance for the "average" assistant (Travailleur Temporaire visa, assistant salary as your only income) — not professional tax advice, and not written by a tax official. Your own situation may differ. When in doubt, book an appointment with your local centre des impôts to confirm anything here before relying on it.

All assistants must file French taxes, including in their first year. Your tax residency is generally considered to be France if your main professional activity is there (more detail here). Don't forget to also file taxes in your home country if required there.

First-year assistants cannot file online — you must file on paper. For the 2026 filing season (covering 2025 income), the paper deadline is May 19, 2026, regardless of where in France you live; your return must be postmarked by this date. Online filing (only available once you have a fiscal number from a previous year) has later, department-specific deadlines, typically late May to early June. Deadlines shift slightly every year, so always confirm the current dates on impots.gouv.fr rather than reusing last year's date.

Hand your paper forms in at the centre des impôts closest to where you were living on December 31 of the tax year — you can also send them by tracked mail, but delivering in person is often more reliable. An appointment usually isn't required, but this can vary by office. After filing, you'll typically receive your fiscal number anywhere from 3–9 months later, which lets you file online the following year — if you visit your local office in person, you can ask them to email you this number sooner.

Haven't filed for a previous year? Some assistants have been able to backfile late returns going back a few years, since France's general statute of limitations for income tax is around three years. Rules and exceptions vary, so treat this as a starting point for a conversation with your local tax office, not a guarantee — ask them directly about your specific situation before assuming you can backfile.

Documents Needed for Your First Declaration

  • A copy of your ID (passport and visa)
  • Your IBAN
  • Your lease (bail), or other proof of housing matching your situation
  • If you're hosted rather than renting: an attestation d'hébergement, plus a copy of your host's ID and a utility bill (e.g. EDF) proving their address
  • If living in a hotel: an attestation from the hotel confirming your stay
  • Your December payslip isn't strictly required, but doesn't hurt to include if you have it

Key Forms

Cerfa 2042 — Main Income Tax Return

The core annual return every assistant files. Covers personal details, your situation, and your income for the year. As a first-time filer, you'll tick the box confirming this is your first declaration rather than entering a fiscal number. If you're single (not married or PACS'd), you only fill in the "déclarant 1" section. Your department code will be 99, used for anyone born outside France. Find the current form at impots.gouv.fr — search "formulaire 2042" for the latest version, since the exact form updates yearly.

Cerfa 3916 — Foreign Bank Accounts

Required for any non-French account you hold — including services like PayPal, Wise, or investment platforms — filed separately for each account, every year. There's a financial penalty for undeclared accounts, so don't skip this if it applies to you. Find the current form at impots.gouv.fr (search "formulaire 3916").

Cerfa 2047 — Foreign Income

Mainly relevant if you're a renewing assistant, to declare income earned in your home country between contracts (for example, income from May–October if you had a gap between two contract years). First-time assistants generally don't need this for income earned before arriving in France. Some countries have tax treaties with France that prevent double taxation — confirm your own country's situation. Find the current form at impots.gouv.fr (search "formulaire 2047").

On the "prélèvement à la source" box (8HV on the main form): this should normally read 0,00 on your payslips, since an assistant's salary typically isn't high enough to have tax withheld at source. Double-check your own payslips rather than assuming this box doesn't apply to you.

Home-country tax obligations vary a lot by nationality, so it's worth checking your own country's rules rather than assuming another country's approach applies to you. As one example: the US has a foreign earned income exclusion that lets qualifying citizens exclude a set amount of foreign-earned income from US tax each year (around $130,000–$132,900 depending on the tax year, adjusted annually for inflation) — since assistant salaries fall well under this, many US assistants owe no US federal tax on this income, though the exact figure and rules should be confirmed each tax season since they change yearly. If you're Ugandan or from another country, a chat with a local tax advisor or your national revenue authority is the safest way to confirm what, if anything, you owe at home.

Paychecks

Access your payslips online at ensap.gouv.fr — many assistants find they can't access this portal, in which case your academy secretary or rectorat can provide copies directly.

  • Check that the "prélèvement à la source" (source deduction) line on your first couple of paychecks shows 0%
  • If you're unsure your pay is correct, comparing notes with other assistants in your academy group chat can help confirm the expected amount
  • If you received two months of salary together (due to a late start) make sure the source deduction reflects your real single-month salary, not the combined amount, or you risk being taxed as though you earn that much every month
  • Any tax overpayment from an error like this gets refunded after you file your tax return

The Prime (End-of-Contract Bonus)

Assistants are generally eligible for a "prime" (bonus) at the end of their contract — roughly 10% of your total program salary, deposited any time between May and September depending on your academy. Ask a mentor or your rectorat for a realistic timeline specific to your academy.

Important: if you close your French bank account before this payment arrives, you won't be able to receive it. Keep your account open until you've confirmed the prime has been paid.

Prime simulator: code.travail.gouv.fr

Unemployment (Chômage)

Assistants are not eligible for unemployment benefits after their contract ends. Unlike standard French employees, assistant paychecks don't include contributions toward unemployment insurance, so there's nothing to draw from afterward. EU citizens should research their own specific eligibility rules, as these may differ.

Getting Help: Your Rights & Unions

Workers have rights in France, and that includes language assistants. Some assistants each year run into real trouble getting paid, receiving payslips, transport reimbursements, or contracts on time. If that happens to you, you're not without options.

A teachers' union, Force Ouvrière, has a contact experienced specifically with language assistant issues: Marie-Laetitia. Since she works within the teachers' section of the union, she can typically respond in your preferred language. The union has branches all over France.

  • Email: snfolc@udfo75.net
  • Phone / WhatsApp: 06 67 98 40 70
  • Website: force-ouvriere.fr
  • Cost to join: around €25/year — membership simply lapses if you don't renew, no exit process needed
Why joining can matter: a Rectorat isn't obliged to meet individually with a language assistant, but it is required to meet with union representatives — and that's often where unresolved issues actually get fixed.

A real example: in one past case, dozens of assistants in the académie de Paris went unpaid for months. Those who joined Force Ouvrière were able to escalate the issue through official channels, and it was eventually resolved — they received their back pay, and changes were made to how assistant paperwork was handled going forward.

Contact details and membership costs can change — confirm the current information directly on the union's website before reaching out.
