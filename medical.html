Practical Life

Medical & Healthcare

General guidance based on common assistant experience — not medical or legal advice. For anything specific to your health or situation, consult a doctor or the Ameli helpline directly.

Carte Vitale & Sécu, Recap

Your Sécu number is your social security number, and it's what allows you to be reimbursed for a portion of your medical costs. The Carte Vitale is the physical card you present at appointments so you don't have to pay the full cost upfront and wait for reimbursement separately. Note: all assistants are registered under La Caisse Primaire d'Assurance Maladie (CPAM) de Paris, regardless of where you actually live or work.

Ameli English-language helpline: +33 9 74 75 36 46

When calling Ameli, it can help to proceed as though you live in Paris when prompted by the automated system (since all assistants are registered there regardless of location) — if you accidentally select another region, just ask to be redirected to Paris. Have your address, phone number, and Secu number ready; they may also ask for your IBAN or its last few digits.

Applying for your Carte Vitale itself happens either online via your Ameli account, or via paperwork mailed to you alongside your permanent Secu number. This can take up to 6 months (sometimes longer, especially for the Paris region) after receiving your temporary number. Many assistants never receive a Carte Vitale during their contract — that's normal, and doesn't stop you from being reimbursed, just means doing it via paper reimbursement forms (Feuille de Soins) instead.

Scam warning: a text message claiming to be from Ameli, or saying your Carte Vitale is ready, with a link to click, is always a scam. Ameli will never contact you by text — your real paperwork arrives by physical mail only.

Feuille de Soins (Reimbursement Paperwork)

Before you have a Carte Vitale, any medical visit or pharmacy purchase should come with a Feuille de Soins — a paper form your doctor or pharmacist fills out, which you send off to be reimbursed. You don't need to wait until you have a Carte Vitale to do this — as soon as you have a Secu number and a French bank account, you can start sending these to your local Assurance Maladie office.

On the form, you'll need to fill in your Secu number, date of birth, address, name, and signature — the rest is completed by your doctor. If it's for medication, keep your prescription to send along with it. Mail to (all assistants, regardless of where you live):

Assurance Maladie de Paris
75948 Paris Cedex 19

Make sure you've declared a médecin traitant (your chosen general doctor) as early as possible — this is a simple form your GP fills out at your first visit, and without it, you won't get the full reimbursement rate on future visits. Form: ameli.fr

As a rough example of how reimbursement works: a standard "secteur 1" doctor visit costs around €25. The Sécu typically reimburses about 70% of that, minus a small fixed deduction (currently €1) — so you'd be reimbursed around €16.50, leaving roughly €8.50 out of pocket if you don't have additional mutuelle coverage. Exact amounts can change, so treat this as illustrative rather than exact.

Mutuelle (Extra Health Insurance)

A mutuelle adds coverage on top of what the Sécu already reimburses. For a short-term stay, many assistants find it's only worth the cost if you have a pre-existing condition requiring regular specialist visits or ongoing medication — otherwise the monthly cost may not be worth it for a single contract year. This becomes more worthwhile if you end up living in France longer term, since routine things like annual dental and vision visits aren't well covered by the Sécu alone.

Recent policy change: as of May 1, 2026, all Education Nationale employees are expected to be enrolled in the MGEN mutuelle unless exempt. Language assistants are generally understood to be exempt, since assistants aren't "titulaires" (permanent civil servants) — but you may still be automatically enrolled by mistake. If you don't want this (paid) coverage, you can request an exemption ("demander une dispense"). Since this is a new rule, confirm directly with MGEN (09 72 72 16 17) and your rectorat rather than relying on this page, as the details may still be settling.

OFII Medical Appointment

After validating your visa, you may be summoned for a medical appointment with OFII (the French immigration office) — though many assistants are never called in for one at all, which is completely normal and nothing to worry about.

If you are summoned, the general process typically includes:

  • Checking in with your passport and summons at the security desk
  • A basic vision check, height and weight measurement (you can ask staff not to state your weight aloud if you'd prefer not to hear it)
  • A chest X-ray in a private area (wear something easy to remove for this)
  • A short consultation with a doctor, who reviews your vaccination records and may discuss general health topics like blood pressure — you can ask to skip topics you're not comfortable discussing, for example by mentioning you're already following up with a doctor at home
  • You'll leave with two copies of a document confirming your appointment — make sure both are stamped before you go, one is for the prefecture and one is for your own records

You may receive an email from OFII requesting documents and scheduling this appointment — it can look suspicious, but is generally legitimate. If in doubt, check the sender's email address carefully, or contact OFII directly to confirm: paris@ofii.fr, helpline 01 85 56 15 55.

Chronic Conditions (ALD)

Certain long-term/chronic conditions ("affection de longue durée," or ALD) qualify for 100% reimbursement from the Sécu. To access this, you'll need your Secu number, then declare a médecin traitant who can apply for ALD benefits on your behalf (this is faster when done online by your doctor rather than by mailed paper form). Approval isn't automatic — Assurance Maladie reviews each request. If approved, download your "attestation des droits" from your Ameli account, print it, and present it at appointments until your Carte Vitale arrives; you may need to remind staff you're covered at 100% for this condition. Once you do get your Carte Vitale, keep it updated regularly at a pharmacy or medical office, or your ALD coverage may stop showing up correctly.

List of qualifying conditions: service-public.fr

General Medical Tips

  • Doctolib is the main appointment-booking site/app used for everything from routine checkups to specialist visits — you'll need a French phone number to create an account, so it's worth setting up soon after you get one
  • Unlike some countries, cancelling a Doctolib appointment last-minute doesn't come with a penalty fee
  • If you have an existing prescription from home, bring it with you (plus a letter from your doctor if possible) — a French doctor can then issue an equivalent prescription, though the exact medication available may differ from what you're used to
  • Specialist appointments can take a few weeks to book, so don't leave anything urgent until the last minute
Reproductive & sexual health: France provides accessible reproductive healthcare services, including contraception and abortion care, through the standard healthcare system. If you need information on accessing these services as a resident, your médecin traitant or a local family planning centre (centre de planification) is the right place to start, and Ameli's helpline can point you toward local services as well.

Healthcare procedures, reimbursement rates, and insurance rules can change — this page reflects common recent experience, not guaranteed current policy. Confirm specifics with Ameli or a healthcare professional.
