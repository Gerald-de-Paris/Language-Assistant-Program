Practical Life

Finding Housing

Housing searches vary a lot by region and luck — this is general guidance and community experience, not a guarantee of how easy or hard yours will be.

Where People Search

Common places assistants look for housing include Leboncoin, SeLoger, and Facebook (with caution — see the scam warnings below). Useful Facebook groups often include local Erasmus groups (student housing turns over often there), "Expats in [city]" groups, or city-specific groups for international residents.

Building Your Dossier

Have your rental dossier ready before visiting apartments — in bigger cities, good places can be snapped up within hours. A typical dossier includes:

  • A copy of your passport and visa
  • A copy of your work contract
  • Your RIB
  • If already in France: your last three payslips, a justificatif de domicile (electricity/internet bill), and your last tax declaration
  • Guarantor information (see Visale below, or your guarantor's last tax declaration, ID copy, and work contract if not using Visale)
Never pay anything before signing a lease. It is illegal in France for a landlord to request payment before the contract (bail) is signed. Anyone asking for money for any reason beforehand — including "to reserve" the apartment — is running a scam. If a price seems too good to be true, or someone tells you a story about why they can't show the apartment in person, treat it as a red flag and walk away.

Renters Insurance

Renters insurance (assurance habitation) is legally required in France, unlike in some countries where it's optional. You'll typically need a signed lease already in place before you can take out a policy, so this isn't something you can arrange ahead of time.

Options assistants commonly use

Some providers require a French bank account (RIB) to sign up; others accept a foreign card instead. Compare a couple of options and check which requirement fits your situation before committing to one.

What to check

Confirm whether payment requires a French RIB, whether there's a mobile app for managing your policy, and how straightforward claims have been for others — prices are typically in the €8–12/month range but change over time.

Before You Move In

  • Confirm exactly what's included in the rent (water, electricity, gas) — don't assume
  • Do a proper "état des lieux" (inventory) with your landlord, noting the condition of appliances, windows, and fittings. Take photos of everything on move-in day and keep them somewhere safe — some landlords won't provide an official inventory document, so having your own record protects you
  • Most landlords require a French bank account before signing a lease — prioritize opening one early, ideally with your Prof Référent or a French-speaking friend along to help

Breaking Your Lease

If you need to end your lease early, you must give written notice (email is usually fine): one month for a furnished apartment, or three months for unfurnished — though exact terms can vary by city, so read your lease fully. A tool to help draft the required notice letter: service-public.fr

Guarantor (Visale)

Visale is a free French government guarantor scheme — if you're unable to pay rent, the government guarantees the landlord will still be paid (you'd still face eviction/legal consequences for non-payment yourself; it protects the landlord, not you from your obligations). It's generally only available to those under 28; other guarantor services exist for those over that age, usually at a cost.

Doing the Visale paperwork on a computer with browser translation turned on can help if your French isn't strong. Once approved, you'll get a document with a date — this is the deadline to sign a lease for Visale to apply, not an expiry of the guarantee itself. Note that Visale usually only covers up to half your income/rent, depending on your situation — confirm with your landlord whether that's acceptable to them.

Other guarantor platforms exist (sometimes at a cost for those over the Visale age limit) — compare options if Visale doesn't fit your situation.

Student & Youth Housing Resources

Many of the following are aimed primarily at students, but are often still worth checking as an assistant. None of these platforms vet listings for scams — that responsibility is always yours:

  • Leboncoin
  • SeLoger
  • La Carte des Colocs (for roommate housing)
  • CROUS (student housing — worth checking if your school reserves rooms for assistants)
  • Lokaviz
  • Studapart
  • Immojeune
  • Appartager (roommate listings)
  • PAP (particulier à particulier, no agency fees)
  • ANIL / your local ADIL (for free, official housing-rights advice by region)

ANIL/ADIL in particular is worth knowing about: it's a free, official service that answers housing-law questions by email or phone, citing the actual legal codes that apply to your situation. Find your local ADIL here.

General Advice

  • It's normal not to have housing sorted before you arrive — many assistants book a short Airbnb or hotel stay to give themselves time to search in person
  • Don't commit to an apartment before knowing your exact placement — some regions are large, and your school may not be in the main city
  • Ask your school if they offer any housing option — you're not obliged to take it, but worth knowing about
  • "Particulier" means renting directly from an owner (no fees); "agence" means an agency is involved (fees apply)
  • Listings marked "CC" (charges comprises) rarely include everything — ask specifically what's covered
  • Consider the cost of furnishing a place if it isn't listed as "meublé" (furnished)
  • Be patient, but proactive — respond quickly to new listings, since good ones move fast
For every place you stay (including informal subleases), try to get an attestation d'hébergement, a copy of the host's ID, and a copy of their bail or a utility bill in their name — even if it feels unnecessary at the time. Missing this documentation for a past address can cause real problems later, particularly at tax time.

Housing platforms, prices, and scam patterns change over time — treat this page as a starting point, and always verify a listing and landlord independently before sending any money or personal documents.
